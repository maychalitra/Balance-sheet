HANZO'S MONEY MAP — PRODUCT SPECIFICATION

1. PRODUCT PURPOSE

Hanzo's Money Map is a cheerful, private balance-sheet website designed for Hanzo, the owner's 11-year-old son. It helps Hanzo record money coming in and going out, understand the balance, and give each euro a simple job. The language must be encouraging and easy to understand without hiding the real ideas of income, expenses, saving, investing, goals, and generosity.

The finished application is one self-contained index.html file. All markup, styles, and JavaScript are inline. It must work as a static page without a server account, database, framework, build step, external font, external image, library, API, or internet connection. The file should also be suitable for a future static Vercel upload.

Currency is fixed to EUR.

2. PRIMARY DASHBOARD

The first screen must show:

- Money I have: total income minus total expenses.
- Money in: the sum of all income entries.
- Money out: the sum of all expense entries.
- A smart money note that changes according to the current data.
- The five-job money plan and cumulative suggested EUR amount for each job.
- Big Goal progress.
- A form for adding an income or expense.
- Hanzo's Monthly Money Story with a weekly running-balance chart.
- A newest-first history of saved money moves.

3. FIVE-JOB MONEY PLAN

Recommended allocation:

- Long-term investment: 20%. Friendly description: money to grow for the future with an adult. The website must explain that investments can rise or fall and must not recommend investment products.
- Big goal: 30%. Money for a specific goal that matters to the child.
- Planned spending: 30%. Purchases the child thinks about before buying.
- Fun now: 10%. Guilt-free money the child can enjoy without overthinking.
- Giving: 10%. Gifts, helping another person, or supporting a chosen cause.

The allocation is guidance calculated from total income; it does not represent five separate bank accounts.

An adult can edit all five percentages in Plan settings. Values must be whole numbers from 0 through 100 and must total exactly 100 before they can be saved. A “Use recommended plan” control restores 20/30/30/10/10 in the settings form.

When a valid income amount is entered, the form previews the exact EUR split before the transaction is saved. Cumulative bucket amounts are calculated from all recorded income. Calculations use euro cents and distribute any rounding remainder so the five displayed amounts always add up exactly to the original income amount.

Example for EUR 10.00:

- Investment: EUR 2.00
- Big goal: EUR 3.00
- Planned spending: EUR 3.00
- Fun: EUR 1.00
- Giving: EUR 1.00

4. BIG GOAL

Plan settings allow a goal name of up to 60 characters and an optional positive target amount with at most two decimal places. When a target exists, progress equals the cumulative Big Goal allocation divided by the target and is capped visually at 100%. The interface shows the allocated amount, target, and rounded percentage. Without a target, it invites the child to set one with an adult.

5. MONEY MOVES

Each transaction contains:

- id: unique non-empty string.
- date: real ISO date in YYYY-MM-DD format, not later than today.
- type: income or expense.
- amount: positive EUR value, maximum EUR 1,000,000,000, with no more than two decimal places.
- category: one recognized category for the selected type.
- note: optional plain text, maximum 120 characters.
- createdAt: numeric creation timestamp used to order entries sharing a date.

Income categories:

- Allowance
- Chore
- Gift
- Sale
- Other

Expense categories:

- Food & Drink
- School
- Transport
- Planned Purchase
- Fun
- Giving
- Goal Purchase
- Other

The child can add, edit, and delete transactions. Editing keeps the original id and creation timestamp. Deleting requires confirmation. The list is sorted by date newest-first, then by creation time, and can be filtered to all moves, income only, or expenses only.

6. MONTHLY MONEY STORY

The dashboard includes “Hanzo's Monthly Money Story,” which combines a monthly overview with weekly chapters:

- The current month opens by default.
- Previous and next arrows move between months. Future months cannot be selected.
- A “This month” button returns from a past month to the current one.
- Three values show income recorded during the selected month, expenses recorded during the selected month, and the total account balance after that month's recorded moves.
- A responsive line chart begins with the balance carried into the month and plots the closing balance after each calendar week.
- The chart has visible week and EUR labels, directly labeled points, a text summary for screen readers, and a clear empty-month message.
- Weeks run Monday through Sunday and are clipped at the beginning and end of the selected month, producing four to six chapters.
- Each weekly chapter shows its date range, money in, money out, and closing balance.
- Expanding a chapter reveals its transactions line by line with date, category, optional note, and signed amount.
- The current week opens by default for the current month. In a past month, the latest week containing entries opens by default.
- Empty weeks say “No money moves this week.”
- A supportive sentence explains whether Hanzo kept more, spent more, broke even, or recorded no moves that month.

The story is calculated from the existing transaction list and does not add fields to the saved-data or backup format.

7. CALCULATIONS AND SMART NOTES

- Total income = sum of all income transactions.
- Total expenses = sum of all expense transactions.
- Current balance = total income minus total expenses.
- All visible money values use clear EUR formatting with two decimal places.

Smart notes are deterministic, supportive, and offline. They should cover at least these situations:

- No entries: invite the first money move.
- Expenses above income: suggest pausing and reviewing the plan with an adult.
- Big Goal fully planned: celebrate the achievement.
- Fun expenses above the Fun allocation guide: encourage reflection without blame.
- Giving recorded: recognize the kind choice.
- Income recorded before spending: celebrate planning first.
- Low overall spending: recognize keeping money for the future.
- General state: ask whether a purchase matters more than the Big Goal.

8. STORAGE, BACKUP, AND SAFETY

The app is intentionally for one child on one browser. It has no account or synchronization.

State is saved as JSON in localStorage using the key kidMoneyTracker.v1. The state shape is:

{
  "version": 1,
  "currency": "EUR",
  "allocation": {
    "investment": 20,
    "goal": 30,
    "planned": 30,
    "fun": 10,
    "giving": 10
  },
  "goal": {
    "name": "",
    "targetAmount": 0
  },
  "transactions": []
}

The app must continue in memory if browser storage is unavailable and clearly warn the user to download a backup before closing.

Backup export downloads a readable, versioned JSON file named hanzo-money-map-backup-YYYY-MM-DD.json. Restore accepts JSON files no larger than 1 MB. Before replacing current data, the complete file must be parsed and validated for supported version, EUR currency, allocation total, goal limits, unique transaction ids, dates, types, amounts, categories, notes, and a maximum of 10,000 transactions. Invalid files must leave existing data untouched and show a clear error. Valid restore requires confirmation.

“Clear everything” requires confirmation and restores the recommended allocation, an empty goal, and an empty transaction list.

User-provided goal names and notes must be inserted as text, never executable HTML.

9. VISUAL AND INTERACTION DESIGN

- Warm notebook-inspired palette with cream paper, dark green text, yellow highlights, and distinct colors for the five jobs.
- Friendly rounded cards, clear grouping, light borders, and restrained shadows.
- Income is green; expenses are coral/red. Color is never the only signal.
- Working dashboard rather than a marketing page: totals and primary controls appear immediately.
- The visible title is “Hanzo's Money Map,” with “Hanzo's balance sheet” as the small brand label.
- Large readable text and touch targets of at least 44 pixels for primary controls.
- Mobile layout stacks panels and transaction details without horizontal scrolling.
- Desktop layout places the money plan beside the transaction form.
- The monthly line chart redraws to remain readable on desktop and mobile, and weekly chapters reflow into compact notebook-style rows.
- Empty, populated, editing, filtered, success, warning, error, storage-unavailable, and confirmation states are included.
- Respect prefers-reduced-motion.

10. ACCESSIBILITY

- Use semantic headings, sections, forms, fieldsets, labels, buttons, and lists.
- Provide a skip link to the tracker.
- Every input has a visible label and every icon-only action has an accessible name.
- Keyboard users can reach and operate every control with a clearly visible focus indicator.
- Validation errors are written in plain language, announced to assistive technology, and focus the field needing attention.
- Success feedback uses a polite live region.
- Big Goal progress exposes an accessible progress value.
- The monthly chart has a concise accessible title and a complete text summary of the starting and weekly balances.
- Weekly chapters use native expandable disclosure controls and remain usable by keyboard.
- Text and essential controls maintain strong contrast.

11. ACCEPTANCE CRITERIA

- Adding income increases income and balance and updates all five allocations.
- Adding an expense increases expenses and decreases balance.
- Editing and deleting recalculate every affected value immediately.
- Date, amount, category, goal, note, and percentage validation work as specified.
- Every income split adds up to the exact original value, including awkward cent amounts.
- Percentages cannot be saved unless they total exactly 100.
- The recommended allocation can be restored.
- Goal progress and each smart-note state display correctly.
- Month navigation, current-month return, and the future-month limit work correctly.
- Monthly income, expense, starting balance, ending balance, weekly totals, and weekly closing balances are correct across month and year boundaries.
- Weekly chapters show the correct transaction lines and useful empty states.
- The chart stays legible and accurately labeled at narrow mobile and wide desktop sizes.
- Entries, settings, and goals survive a normal refresh when localStorage is available.
- Exported data can be restored; malformed or incompatible data cannot overwrite current data.
- Clearing data requires explicit confirmation.
- The page works at narrow mobile and wide desktop widths, with keyboard navigation and useful screen-reader text.
- Opening index.html provides the complete application and no external request is required.

12. DELIVERY

Deliver index.html as the only application file and this spec.txt as its documentation. Keep the site local for now. Do not deploy it. A future Vercel deployment can serve index.html as a static root page without application changes.
